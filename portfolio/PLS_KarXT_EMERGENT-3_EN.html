Plain Language Summary

EMERGENT-3 — A study of KarXT in adults hospitalised with an acute episode of schizophrenia.

Karen Guimarães Hoyer · AI-assisted English adaptation

Phase 3 Registry NCT04738123 Protocol KAR-009
01

Study information

This independent writing sample explains the published EMERGENT-3 results for a general audience. It was not commissioned or approved by the study sponsor.

  • Study: Efficacy and Safety of Xanomeline-Trospium Chloride in Schizophrenia: A Randomized Clinical Trial.
  • Study medicine: Xanomeline–trospium chloride, also called KarXT.
  • Registry number: NCT04738123.
  • Sponsor protocol number: KAR-009.
  • Study sponsor: Karuna Therapeutics.
  • Sponsor contact: The August 2021 study protocol records the address as 33 Arch Street, Suite 3110, Boston, MA 02110. Its current validity has not been verified.

EMERGENT-3 was part of the EMERGENT clinical trial programme. It tested whether KarXT reduced symptoms compared with placebo, and recorded side effects during five weeks of treatment in hospital.

02

Why was the study carried out?

Schizophrenia is a long-term mental health condition. Most antipsychotic medicines act by blocking particular dopamine receptors in the brain. They help many people, but some continue to have symptoms or experience difficult side effects, such as weight gain, sleepiness or movement problems.

KarXT uses a different approach. It acts on receptors involved in another chemical signalling system, called the muscarinic system, without directly blocking dopamine D2 receptors.

Action in the brain

Xanomeline

Activates muscarinic receptors in the brain. The study tested whether this approach could reduce symptoms of psychosis.

Action outside the brain

Trospium chloride

Acts mainly outside the brain. It was combined with xanomeline to reduce some unwanted effects of muscarinic receptor activation elsewhere in the body. Side effects can still occur.

Schizophrenia can involve:

  • hearing or seeing things that others do not
  • strong beliefs that are not shared by others and are not supported by evidence
  • difficulty organising thoughts
  • social withdrawal and reduced motivation

This study involved people experiencing acute psychosis: a period of worsening symptoms that required hospital treatment.

03

Who took part?

The study randomly assigned 256 adults aged 18 to 65 with schizophrenia to receive KarXT or placebo.

  • Age and sex: The average age was about 43 years. About 75% of participants were men.
  • Race: About 61% were Black or African American and 38% were White.
  • Location: The study took place at 30 inpatient centres in the United States and Ukraine. Recruitment in Ukraine stopped after the war began in February 2022.

Participants had to have schizophrenia with an acute worsening that required hospital treatment and meet specified symptom-severity criteria. People with a history of resistance to antipsychotic treatment were excluded.

People with another primary psychiatric disorder in the previous year, or substantial symptom improvement between screening and the start of treatment, were also excluded.

These results describe the people who met the study's entry criteria. They do not necessarily apply to everyone with schizophrenia.

04

How was the study conducted?

The study was randomised, double-blind and placebo-controlled. Participants were assigned by chance to one of two groups. Participants and study staff did not know which treatment each person received. These methods help reduce the influence of expectations on the comparison.

  • Groups: Each participant had an equal chance of receiving KarXT or placebo, a capsule without the study medicine.
  • Duration: Treatment lasted five weeks in hospital. The study ran from April 2021 to December 2022.
  • Dose: KarXT doses started low and increased according to the study schedule and how well each person tolerated treatment.
  • Assessment: Researchers used the Positive and Negative Syndrome Scale, or PANSS, to rate symptoms. Lower scores mean less severe symptoms.
05

What were the main results?

  • After five weeks, the average PANSS score fell by 20.6 points with KarXT and 12.2 points with placebo. The difference was 8.4 points in favour of KarXT. The study's main statistical test supported this difference.
  • The study also evaluated how many people achieved a defined level of symptom improvement. Under statistical rules established before the study, this outcome was not formally tested because an earlier outcome did not meet the required threshold. It therefore cannot be used to conclude that the medicine provided a benefit. The full results are available in the scientific article.
  • A difference between groups was observed from the second week of treatment.
  • KarXT reduced “positive” symptoms, such as hallucinations and delusions, more than placebo. For “negative” symptoms, such as reduced motivation or social withdrawal, the week-five comparison did not meet the required statistical threshold.

Group averages do not predict how much an individual person will improve.

06

What side effects were reported?

Digestive side effects were among the most common. According to the authors, these were generally mild or moderate and often temporary.

Selected events during five weeks of treatment. Safety groups: 125 people receiving KarXT and 128 receiving placebo. Frequencies are expressed per 1,000 people to make the proportions easier to compare; the study did not include 1,000 people in each group.

  • Heart rate: An increase peaked around day eight, at about nine additional beats per minute compared with placebo.
  • Weight, sleepiness and movement: Over the five-week study, these measures were broadly similar between groups. This does not establish the absence of these risks during longer treatment.

The table shows selected events, not every reported event. An event occurring during treatment does not necessarily mean that the medicine caused it.

07

What does this study not tell us?

Limitations
  • Treatment lasted only five weeks, so the study cannot establish long-term benefits or risks.
  • KarXT was compared with placebo, not another antipsychotic medicine.
  • Participants were hospitalised adults who met specific entry criteria.
  • The study does not show whether KarXT is better for people who have not responded to other antipsychotics.
  • The study was funded by Karuna Therapeutics, and several authors were company employees.
08

What do the findings mean?

In this study, adults receiving xanomeline–trospium had a greater average reduction in schizophrenia symptoms than adults receiving placebo over five weeks.

The findings support a different approach to treating schizophrenia. They do not establish superiority over other antipsychotic medicines or determine which treatment is best for a particular person.

Main message

KarXT improved the main symptom measure compared with placebo. Digestive side effects were more common with KarXT. Longer-term benefits, risks and individual treatment choices require additional evidence and clinical assessment.

At the time of the 2024 publication, two longer-term studies, EMERGENT-4 and EMERGENT-5, were ongoing. Both followed outpatients receiving the medicine for 52 weeks. This describes their status at publication, not their current status.

See the EMERGENT-3 study record for further information.
